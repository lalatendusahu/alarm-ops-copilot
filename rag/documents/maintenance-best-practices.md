# Maintenance Best Practices

## Vibration Analysis Cadence

Critical rotating assets (high-criticality pumps, compressors, and turbines) should receive a
full vibration spectrum analysis quarterly, and immediately following any bearing replacement or
alignment work as a baseline check. A single RMS trip alarm is sufficient to trigger an alarm,
but is not sufficient on its own to diagnose the failure mode -- always pair it with spectral
analysis before ordering parts.

## Bearing Replacement Follow-Up

After any bearing replacement, take a baseline vibration reading within 48 hours and again after
two weeks of run time. If vibration trends back upward within 60 days of a bearing replacement,
treat it as a new investigation rather than a warranty issue with the part -- common causes
include inadequate alignment during reinstallation, an unaddressed imbalance, or a bearing that
was replaced without addressing the original root cause.

## Work Order Prioritization

- **High priority**: safety-related findings, or a recurring alarm on a high-criticality asset.
- **Medium priority**: findings on medium-criticality assets, or preventive work identified
  during routine inspection.
- **Low priority**: cosmetic or non-functional findings.

## Closing Out Work Orders

A work order addressing a recurring alarm should not be closed as "completed" until the alarm
history shows the condition has actually stopped recurring, not merely that the physical repair
was performed. If the alarm recurs after the work order is closed, open a new work order that
references the original for traceability rather than editing the closed record.
