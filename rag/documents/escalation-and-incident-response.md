# Escalation and Incident Response

## Escalation Triggers

- A critical-severity alarm that remains unacknowledged for more than 5 minutes.
- Any alarm flood (see Alarm Flood Response Procedure).
- A recurring high or critical alarm on a high-criticality asset that has already had one
  corrective work order completed without resolving the underlying pattern.
- Any condition posing an immediate safety risk (see Rotating Equipment Safety Instructions).

## Escalation Path

1. **Shift supervisor** -- first point of escalation for any operational alarm condition.
2. **Reliability / maintenance engineer** -- engaged when a recurring or rationalization-flagged
   alarm requires root-cause investigation or a new work order.
3. **Unit engineer or plant manager** -- engaged for safety-impacting conditions or when a
   repeated failure suggests a design or process issue rather than a maintenance issue.

## Escalation Summary Content

An escalation summary should include: the asset and alarm involved, the time window and
occurrence count, prior maintenance history for the same failure mode, the priority score if
available, and a recommended next action. Escalations without a recommended next action are
considered incomplete.

## Documentation

All escalations must be logged with a timestamp and the identity of the person escalating and
receiving. Verbal-only escalations for critical conditions must be followed by a written summary
within the same shift.
